The Skill Suite of Horrors · third season

Lost in Translation and other catastrophes

A night in six cheat sheets and an encore. The third program of the Skill Suite of Horrors.

Between what a human says and what a model understands, there is always a translator. Sometimes it's speech recognition, sometimes a German Windows, sometimes a technical term that somebody took literally. The third program is about these translators and the small catastrophes that live inside them. All the stories really happened. The cheat sheets really help.

The door measures nobody against the frame.

They greet you like old acquaintances.

Number 17

gobbledygook (kauderwelsch)

Windows speaks its owner's language.

Can
read SIDs instead of “Jeder” (Everyone), error codes instead of German messages, and numbers regardless of region.
When
when a script does its sums differently on a German Windows.
Background
The same line reads “1.5” as fifteen in Berlin and gives up in Vienna.
get it
Number 18

back-to-front (rueckwaerts)

Identical can also mean identically wrong.

Can
write copy commands so that they only go one way.
When
before Copy-Item, robocopy, ln and mklink.
Background
2:42 a.m., a board shrinks from 504 to 34 KB, and the check reports “identical”.
get it

when a message could mean more than one thing.

“Du musst gerade resumen.” (You just have to resume.) Not a typo. A house word.
